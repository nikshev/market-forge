## Context

Every chain capture tool in this repository verifies a decode against the
deployed contract. [[REQ-WP-047]] checked Uniswap v4 pool ids against the
singleton's own `Initialize` log, [[REQ-WP-046]] checked Curve quotes against
the pools' own `get_dy`. The method has been reliable enough that it stopped
being written down, which is why it is written down here.

**It does not work on HyperEVM.** The three public endpoints this project uses
for chain 999 are not archive nodes, and they do not say so. Asked for
`slot0()` at a block a few hundred behind the head they answer — with near-head
state. Measured on 2026-09-12 at the same pool:

    block 45722077   slot0().sqrtPriceX96 = 709887953602415250851994
    block 45722078   slot0().sqrtPriceX96 = 709887953602415250851994
    block 45722079   slot0().sqrtPriceX96 = 709887953602415250851994

while the `Swap` logs in those same blocks report 709911150171231978567005 and
709910689433504773053979. The state is not frozen on chain; it is frozen in the
answer.

**The failure is silent by construction.** There is no error, no empty result
and no `missing trie node`. What comes back is a well-formed price a few basis
points from the truth — positive, ordered, and believable, which is the shape
this project keeps meeting. A decoder checked against it would have been
declared correct while being checked against nothing.

The two-endpoint quorum from [[REQ-WP-046]] is what caught it. Asked for the
same historical `slot0()`, `rpc.hyperliquid.xyz` and `rpc.hypurrscan.io`
returned different prices and `EndpointPool.call` refused rather than picking
one. A single-endpoint capture would have recorded the wrong number with no
indication anything had happened.

## Decision

**Nothing in the HyperEVM capture verifies a decode against historical contract
state.** State is read only at the scan's own head block, and only for what a
contract immutably is — which family it belongs to, its tokens, its tick
spacing — never for what it was at the moment of a log.

**The oracle is the transaction's own ERC-20 `Transfer` logs.** A swap's
`amount0` and `amount1` are the pool's net movement of each token, and the
transfers that prove it are in the same transaction as the swap. Logs are
served correctly and identically by all three endpoints; this needs no archive
node and no trust in one.

The rule is narrower than "net the transaction", because a transaction holds
more than one operation: captured swaps sit in transactions where the same pool
also flashes or collects, and netting across the whole receipt disagrees with
the event by orders of magnitude. **The transfers that
settle a swap are the nearest preceding transfer of each token involving the
pool.** Every one of the 164 moving sides of the 83 captured swaps
reconciles under that rule, across both protocols.

Two sides moved nothing. A swap can report `amount1 = 0` -- token0 paid in and
nothing out -- and then no transfer settles that side at all. The nearest
preceding transfer of that token is the *previous* operation's, and a check
that accepted it would have reported a mismatch of entirely plausible size. So
a zero side is recorded as unverifiable rather than matched, and the count of
them is asserted, so the exclusion cannot quietly grow.

## Consequences

The fixture is self-verifying offline: `tests/fixtures/hyperevm/swaps.jsonl`
carries each swap with the raw logs and the transfers of its transaction, so
the reconciliation is a unit test rather than a capture-time assertion nobody
re-runs. The capture takes an explicit block range so a fixture that needs a
new field can be re-recorded over the same blocks, leaving every number quoted
here unchanged.

**What this costs is pool state.** §18.12.2's `LiquidityState` reconstruction
needs tick-level state at a historical block, and on this chain no public
endpoint will serve it. That is not a gap in [[REQ-WP-058]]; it is a
prerequisite an archive node has to supply, and it belongs in the Phase 4
`not_delivered` list where the `LiquidityState` table already sits.

The deployed contract remains the arbiter on chains where one can be read at
the block in question, and [[REQ-WP-046]]'s and [[REQ-WP-047]]'s captures are
unaffected. What this adds is that
**an endpoint answering a historical call is not evidence that it read
historical state**, and a quorum of two is what turns that from an assumption
into a check.

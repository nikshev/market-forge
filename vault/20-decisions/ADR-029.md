## Context

PRD §23.6 is unambiguous about the bar a GMDH result has to clear:

> Every GMDH result must beat:
> - no-skill base rate;
> - logistic regression;
> - regularized logistic regression;
> - simple decision tree;
> - gradient boosted trees;
> - optionally LightGBM/XGBoost if dependency allowed.

Three of those are tree models. Implementing a decision tree and a gradient
boosting ensemble to a standard worth comparing against is a substantial piece
of work in its own right — and implementing them badly is worse than not
having them, because a GMDH model that beats a poor tree looks validated.

The alternative is a dependency. The PRD leaves the door open ("if dependency
allowed") for LightGBM and XGBoost specifically, and scikit-learn would supply
all three. That is a decision with consequences for the build, the CI image and
the project's dependency surface, and it is not one this work package should
make quietly.

## Decision

**Two baselines run: the no-skill base rate and logistic regression**, the
latter implemented over NumPy, which is already a dependency.

**The remaining three are named in every report as not run.** The report
carries the full §23.6 list with a status against each, so a reader sees the
gap rather than assuming the list was satisfied.

## Amended

Amended by [[ADR-050]] (2026-09-09). [[REQ-EXP-008]] required the regularized
logistic and the boosted trees, and both were built in a way that answers the
objections below rather than overriding them: the elastic net's strengths are
required arguments with no defaults, and the trees are depth-limited by
construction so they can represent an interaction. Two baselines remain not run,
with their reasons.

## Consequences

- **A GMDH result here is not yet "validated" in §23.6's sense**, and the
  report says so on its face. That is the point: a comparison against two of
  six baselines is a partial comparison, and a report that omitted the other
  four would read as a complete one.
- **The base rate is the baseline that matters most and is the cheapest.** Most
  models that fail, fail against it — a classifier on an imbalanced target
  that predicts the majority class scores well on accuracy and knows nothing.
- **Regularized logistic regression is one argument away** from the plain one
  and is deliberately left out rather than faked: an L2 term with an
  unvalidated coefficient is not the baseline §23.6 asks for.
- **The dependency question is left open, on purpose.** When a tree baseline is
  genuinely needed, adding scikit-learn or LightGBM is a decision to make with
  its cost visible, not a side effect of a feature branch.

## Context

`ObjectStore` is documented as "what the table layer needs from durable storage,
and nothing more": `put`, `put_if_absent`, `get`, `list`. It has no `delete`,
and that absence has been load-bearing. The canonical plane is append-only (PRD
§0.5), the table layer's atomicity rests on `put_if_absent`, and nothing in the
commit path has ever had a reason to remove an object.

[[REQ-WP-038]] needs one. Retention is the only operation in this system that
destroys, and it cannot be written against a port that cannot delete.

Three shapes were available.

**Add `delete` to `ObjectStore`.** Shortest. It also hands the commit path an
operation it has no use for, in a codebase whose central claim is that finalized
records are never rewritten. The protection today is that `Table` *cannot*
delete; after this change the protection would be that `Table` *does not*, which
is a promise about discipline rather than a fact about a type. Nothing would
fail on the day someone added a "clean up the failed commit" branch, and that
branch is a natural thing to reach for.

**Give retention a store of its own.** A second implementation, constructed
separately, holding the delete. It removes the risk and duplicates the S3 client
setup, the error mapping and the prefix handling — and the two would drift, with
the retention path being the one nobody exercises until it matters.

**Express the capability in the type.** A narrower protocol that extends
`ObjectStore` with `delete`. The concrete stores satisfy both. A function that
prunes asks for the wider one; every existing signature keeps asking for the
narrower one and therefore keeps being unable to delete.

## Decision

A `PrunableObjectStore` protocol extending `ObjectStore` with `delete`.
`InMemoryObjectStore` and `S3ObjectStore` implement it; `Table`, `back_up`,
`restore` and `verify` continue to take `ObjectStore` and are structurally
incapable of removing anything.

## Consequences

The commit path's inability to delete survives the arrival of a deleter, and it
survives it as a type error rather than as a code review. Adding a removal to
`Table` now requires widening its own signature, which is a visible change in a
diff and a deliberate act rather than a convenient one.

The cost is a second protocol name for what is physically one store, and a
reader meeting `PrunableObjectStore` has to learn why the split exists. That is
a real cost and it is the cheaper half of the trade: the alternative cost is
paid once, quietly, by whoever adds the cleanup branch.

This does not make retention safe. It makes retention the only place where being
unsafe is possible, which is where the requirement's other rules — pins, the
newest snapshot, the removal order — can be concentrated and tested.

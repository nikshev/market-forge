## Context

[[ADR-029]] ran two of PRD §23.6's six baselines and named the other four as not
run. Its two reasons were specific:

> Regularized logistic regression is one argument away from the plain one and is
> deliberately left out rather than faked: an L2 term with an unvalidated
> coefficient is not the baseline §23.6 asks for.

> Implementing them badly is worse than not having them, because a GMDH model
> that beats a poor tree looks validated.

REQ-EXP-008 requires them: "logistic, elastic net, gradient boosting, and GMDH
are evaluated on the same point-in-time feature matrix". Reporting two of the
four as unavailable would leave the experiment unmet, and the experiment is the
thing that decides whether GMDH is worth keeping.

## Decision

**Both are built, and each answers ADR-029's objection directly.**

**The elastic net's penalty and L1 ratio are required arguments.** ADR-029's
objection was never the arithmetic — it is forty lines — but the coefficient,
which a default would choose on the researcher's behalf and then hide. A test
reads the signature and asserts there is no default. Where the comparison report
needs a pair to run with, it declares its own as the *report's* research
defaults, in the report's own module, rather than in the model's.

**The boosted trees are depth-limited by construction, not by default.** A
booster of depth-one stumps is additive in the features: it cannot represent an
interaction, which is the only reason §23.6 asks for a tree at all. That is
precisely ADR-029's "poor tree" — a baseline a GMDH result beats by finding
something the baseline structurally cannot see. The default depth is two, the
shallowest that can hold an interaction, and a test asserts the booster beats a
linear model on an interaction target by a wide margin.

**Two baselines remain not run**, with their reasons in every report: the single
decision tree, because the ensemble covers what §23.6 wants a tree for and a
lone tree is a weaker version of the same question rather than a different one;
and LightGBM/XGBoost, because that is still a dependency decision.

## Consequences

- **A GMDH result now clears four of six**, and the report still says which two
  it did not.
- **The dependency question stays open**, which was ADR-029's other point. These
  implementations are textbook and say so; they are baselines, not models to
  deploy.
- **The tree can overfit a small fold**, which is why it carries a minimum leaf
  size, tested by making it larger than the sample and asserting the ensemble
  goes flat.
- **Both are deterministic without a seed**: the splits are exhaustive over
  observed midpoints and the descent is fixed. Principle XI holds without an
  argument about seeding.

## Context

[[REQ-WP-022]] built PRD §23.9's registration and gave it a validation rule:
the validation span may not start before the training span ends. The reasoning
cited PRD §41 rule 10 — a model validated on rows it was trained on is not
validated.

[[REQ-WP-024]] then wrote the first real registrations and could not satisfy it.
The folds a study runs on are walk-forward, and they interleave by construction:

    fold 0: train [0, 13s]   validate [24s, 47s]
    fold 1: train [0, 37s]   validate [48s, 71s]
    fold 2: train [0, 61s]   validate [72s, 95s]

Fold 1 trains on data later than fold 0 validated on. Any run-level span pair
therefore overlaps, and the rule refused it.

What happened next is the part worth recording. Rather than question the rule,
the run wrote `1/2/2/3` into the four fields and the outcome note called it
"shape rather than truth". A registry whose entire purpose is to describe an
artifact truthfully carried four invented numbers, and it passed every test and
a green CI.

## Decision

**The non-overlap rule applies to a single split and to nothing else.**
`Registration` gains `validation_regime`, with no default, and enforces the rule
only for `single_split`.

Three things are true and only the first two were noticed when the rule was
written:

- for a **single split**, the spans are the whole of what happened, so an
  overlap between them *is* the leak;
- within any **fold**, training and validation never touch — and that is the
  property PRD §41 rule 10 is about;
- across the folds of a walk-forward run, the outer extents overlap, and that
  is a fact about the schedule rather than about leakage.

**The rule was over-reach, not protection.** §23.9 asks for four spans and says
nothing about their order. Rule 10 is about a locked test segment, which
walk-forward satisfies per fold. And the guarantee the rule claimed to provide
already existed in two better places: `WalkForwardFolds` constructs the purge and
embargo, and `certify` refuses a dataset that fails the leakage checks. The
registration was duplicating a guarantee with a cruder test that a correct scheme
legitimately fails.

`validation_regime` has no default, for [[ADR-015]]'s reason: a field with one is
a field an author can forget to think about, and this one decides which rule
applies.

## Consequences

- `run_study` writes the folds' true extents, and they overlap. A test asserts
  the overlap rather than tolerating it, so the next person to tighten the rule
  will see why it is loose.
- **A guard was weakened, deliberately, and this is where that is on record.**
  The protection did not move to a weaker place; it was always in the fold
  builder and the certificate, and the registration was restating it.
- A registration now says how it was validated, which a reader of §23.9's spans
  needs in order to interpret them at all.
- **The near-miss is the lesson.** The false spans were introduced by the same
  author who wrote the rule, in the same session, and survived a mutation sweep
  and a green CI — because nothing tested that a registration's spans were
  *true*, only that they were accepted. A value nobody checks against reality is
  a value that will eventually be invented.

## Alternatives considered

- **Register per fold**, where every span pair is clean. Rejected: the artifact
  a result cites is the variant across its folds ([[REQ-WP-024]]), so per-fold
  registrations would describe artifacts nothing cites, and the run's own
  identity would be ambiguous between them.
- **Keep the rule and leave the placeholders.** Rejected on sight once stated
  plainly: it trades a real, readable field for a rule that was not asked for.

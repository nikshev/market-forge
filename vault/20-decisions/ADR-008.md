## Context

PRD describes the signal lifecycle twice, in two vocabularies.

§21.2 gives a generic sequence: `NONE → APPROACH → TOUCH → REJECTION_PENDING →
CONFIRMED → ALERTED → RESOLVED / INVALIDATED / EXPIRED`.

§21.1 gives per-family sequences with different names for what appear to be the
same steps: `APPROACHING_UPPER`, `TOUCH_UPPER`, `REJECTED_BACK_INSIDE`,
`SIGNAL_SHORT`.

Separately, §21.1 defines six signal families. REQ-WP-007 asks for "long/short
boundary + middle setups", which is families A through D. E and F — breakout and
breakdown with retest — are a different shape: they fire when the channel is
*failing* rather than holding, and §21.1 has them transitioning or invalidating
the channel itself.

## Decision

**§21.2's vocabulary is the state machine.** §21.1's per-family names are read as
prose describing the same lifecycle from one family's point of view, not as a
second set of states. Direction and boundary are attributes of a candidate, not
part of its state name.

**WP-007 implements families A, B, C and D.** E and F are out of scope and get
their own requirement.

## Consequences

- **One vocabulary means one state machine, not four.** Encoding
  `APPROACHING_UPPER` and `APPROACHING_LOWER` as distinct states would double
  the transition table for every family added, and the transitions are identical
  — only the comparison direction differs. Deterministic transitions, which is
  what REQ-WP-007 actually asks for, are far easier to prove over one table.
- **`SIGNAL_SHORT` becomes `CONFIRMED` plus a direction.** A consumer asking
  "is this a short?" reads a field; a consumer asking "has it been confirmed?"
  reads the state. Conflating the two makes both questions harder.
- **Excluding E and F keeps this reviewable.** Breakout-and-retest depends on
  channel transition and invalidation semantics that §21.1 only sketches, and
  building them here would mean specifying a channel lifecycle this requirement
  does not own.
- **The zone boundaries are PRD §13.11's defaults**, and that section says
  plainly they are "research defaults, not proven parameters". They are
  configuration, and nothing here treats them as findings.
- Reversible: adding E and F later adds preconditions and a trigger, not a new
  vocabulary. If per-family states turn out to be necessary, the direction field
  is where that distinction already lives.

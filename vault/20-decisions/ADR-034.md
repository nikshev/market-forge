## Context

PRD §18.6 ends with an instruction that reads oddly until you separate its two
halves: "If an implementation changes unexpectedly, ingestion must fail closed
for semantic state reconstruction while continuing to retain raw logs."

Two failure modes are being ruled out at once.

**Guessing.** A decoder that matched on event signature alone would happily
decode a proxy that has been upgraded to different semantics. The events it
produced would be structurally valid and economically wrong, and nothing
downstream could tell.

**Dropping.** Refusing the log entirely loses the only evidence of what
happened, and the contract upgrade is exactly the moment that evidence matters
most — someone will need to reconstruct the gap once a decoder exists.

## Decision

**Decoding fails closed**: a log whose registry entry does not match on ABI
hash and deployment range produces no event, and the failure is recorded with
the reason.

**Raw retention is unconditional**: the envelope is stored whether or not
anything could decode it.

Selection is by protocol, version and block range, so two decoder versions for
one contract are chosen deterministically rather than by import order.

## Consequences

- **A contract upgrade produces a visible gap, not wrong data.** Decoded events
  stop; raw logs continue; the reason is recorded. Someone notices because the
  feature stops, which is a much better signal than a feature that keeps
  working incorrectly.
- **Backfilling after the fact is possible**, because the raw logs were kept.
  That is the half of the rule that would be easy to skip, and it is the half
  that makes the fail-closed affordable.
- **The registry is now the thing that must be maintained.** A missing entry
  looks exactly like an upgraded contract, and both stop decoding. That is the
  correct conflation: in both cases we do not know what the log means.
- **This mirrors [[ADR-022]]'s shape.** There, a transform declares whether it
  looks forward and the engine refuses the ones that do. Here a decoder
  declares what it understands and the registry refuses the logs it does not.

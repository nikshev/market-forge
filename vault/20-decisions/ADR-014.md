title: A wall's size decrease is executed up to what traded there, cancelled beyond it
status: accepted

## Context

PRD §15.6's `WallState` carries `executed_size_est` and `cancelled_size_est`.
The `_est` is the PRD's own: the book does not say why size left a level. A
resting 40 that becomes a resting 10 lost 30, and the book alone cannot say
whether it traded or was pulled.

The distinction is the whole point of tracking walls. PRD §2.2 warns that large
passive orders can vanish quickly, so a static heatmap creates a false sense of
liquidity. A wall that was eaten and a wall that was pulled tell opposite
stories about the same picture.

## Decision

For each observation interval, at each tracked price:

```
executed  = min(size_decrease, volume_traded_at_that_price_in_the_interval)
cancelled = size_decrease - executed
```

Trades are attributed to the price they printed at, not to a band.

## Consequences

- **Executed is bounded by something that actually happened.** The trade
  stream is evidence; the book difference is not. Attributing more to execution
  than the tape shows would manufacture liquidity consumption from an
  observation gap.
- **The bias is towards reporting cancellation, and that is the safer error.**
  When trades are missing or late, a decrease is attributed to cancellation.
  Under PRD §2.2's warning, over-reporting cancellation makes the book look
  less trustworthy than it is; over-reporting execution would make a pulled
  wall look like absorbed demand, which is the failure that costs money.
- **Both numbers are estimates and are named as such.** The registry entries
  say so, and their `point_in_time_safe` field is true — they use only trades
  and book states at or before `t`.
- **A trade at a price with no tracked wall is simply not attributed.** It
  counts in volume features; it does not create a wall retroactively.
- **Coarse observation intervals blur the split.** Two walls at the same price
  within one interval are one decrease and one attribution. The interval is the
  book's own update cadence, so this is as fine as the data allows.
- Reversible: the rule is one function, and the raw decrease is retained.

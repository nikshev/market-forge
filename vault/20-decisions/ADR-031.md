## Context

PRD §41 rule 9 — "Fees/slippage must be included in economic evaluation" — has
been deferred twice, and both times for the same honest reason:

- [[ADR-009]] scoped returns out of the backtest entirely, because there was no
  outcome definition and no fill model, and a gross figure would be quoted as
  the figure.
- [[ADR-024]] left the rule at `draft` because nothing produced an economic
  number for costs to be included in.

REQ-WP-020 changes that. Its acceptance requires an out-of-sample report with
"realized `R`", and §44A.24 defines R precisely. So an economic evaluation now
exists — which means rule 9 applies to it, and applies now.

§44A.23 supplies the shape: "A stop price is not a guaranteed fill price", with
`requested_stop_price`, `trigger_price`, `first_executable_price`,
`realized_exit_price` and `stop_slippage_bps` to be stored, and "Risk metrics
use realized executable exit when available, not ideal stop price."

## Decision

**Realized R is computed net of fees and modelled slippage**, from the first
executable price rather than the requested stop.

**Slippage is a configurable basis-point cost applied on the adverse side** —
against the position, always. It is *modelled*, not measured, and the field is
named so.

**REQ-BIAS-009 advances to `implemented`**, scoped to this evaluation. Its rule
binds any future economic metric too, and the next one has this precedent to
follow rather than a deferral to inherit.

## Consequences

- **The comparison against naive baselines becomes meaningful.** A fixed-percent
  trailing stop moves more often than a structural one; without costs its extra
  activity is free, and it wins on gross R for a reason that would not survive
  contact with an exchange.
- **Modelled slippage is not real slippage, and the naming says so.** A
  constant basis-point cost understates a gap and overstates a calm fill. The
  honest version needs the book at stop time — REQ-WP-004 supplies a book, but
  no fill model exists, and inventing one here would be [[ADR-009]]'s mistake
  in a different place.
- **The adverse-side convention is deliberate.** Slippage that sometimes
  favoured the position would be a rounding argument; on a stop it is always
  the wrong way, and modelling it symmetrically would flatter every policy
  equally and change none of the comparisons that matter.
- **ADR-009 still stands for the backtest.** REQ-WP-010 reports no economic
  metric because it has no outcome definition; this feature has one, supplied
  by the position's own initial risk. The two are not in tension: rule 9 says
  costs must be included *in an economic evaluation*, not that one must exist.

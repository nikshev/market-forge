## Context

PRD §13.1 names `slope_normalized` as a field and §46's WP-006 lists "normalized
slope" as a requirement. Neither says what it is normalized by. Searching the
document finds only those two mentions.

PRD §13.9 lists nine submetrics for `channel_quality` and says the initial
formula should be a transparent weighted average. Two of the nine cannot be
computed by this feature: `forecast_calibration_score` needs the forecast
channel of §13.8, and `regime_compatibility` needs the regime engine of §20.
Neither exists.

## Decision

**`slope_normalized` is the OLS slope divided by the residual standard
deviation**, both in log space: `b / sigma_residual`. Dimensionless, and
therefore comparable across symbols and timeframes.

**`channel_quality` is computed from the six submetrics whose inputs exist**,
as a weighted average with configurable weights: coverage, touch consistency,
slope stability, width stability, residual structure penalty, and outlier
penalty. `forecast_calibration_score`, `regime_compatibility` and `age_score`
are omitted, and their absence is reported rather than silently treated as
neutral.

## Consequences

- **Slope in units of noise is what a scanner needs.** PRD §1.1 describes
  scanning many markets and ranking them by setup quality. A slope in log-price
  per bar is not comparable between a symbol trading at 78,000 and one at 0.4;
  divided by its own residual spread, it answers "is this trend large relative
  to how noisy this channel is", which is the question ranking actually asks.
  The alternative — normalizing by channel width — measures nearly the same
  thing, since width is built from the same residuals; the ratio is simpler and
  its units are stated.
- **A quality score built from six of nine parts must say so.** Reporting a
  number in `[0,1]` that silently omits a third of its definition would invite
  comparison against a later, fuller score as though they meant the same thing.
  The snapshot therefore records which submetrics contributed, and the omitted
  ones are absent rather than defaulted to a neutral value — a default would
  quietly move the score toward the middle and nobody would know why.
- **Weights are configuration** (PRD §0.12), so adding the missing three later
  changes a configuration rather than the formula's shape.
- **This is a baseline, and its numbers are not yet evidence of anything.** PRD
  §13.9 says ML scoring comes later and §48 lists the research questions that
  must be answered before any of this drives a decision. Fixing the weights now
  is a starting point for measurement, not a claim about what matters.
- Reversible: both definitions live in one module, and the snapshot records the
  model version, so channels computed under a different definition remain
  distinguishable.

## Context

§18.14 defines the comparison unit: `buy_exec_px(N)`, `sell_exec_px(N)`,
`all_in_cost_bps(N)`, with the CEX side made of spread, fee and book impact and
the DEX side of AMM fee, price impact, gas and an optional MEV margin.

The engine could compute those itself. It has the pieces —
`depth_within_bps` from REQ-WP-004 and `depth_to_bps` from REQ-WP-015 — and
folding them in would make a single call answer "which venue is cheapest".

That would be a mistake, for a reason this repository has already met twice.
Fees, gas and MEV margins are venue-specific and time-varying operational
inputs. An engine that computed all-in cost would have to hold assumptions
about all of them, and those assumptions would be invisible at the call site —
the same failure [[ADR-016]] avoided by omitting the alert's score rather than
inventing one, and [[ADR-031]] avoided by making slippage an explicit, named,
modelled input rather than a constant buried in a helper.

## Decision

**The engine takes executable quotes and compares them.** A caller supplies,
per venue, the executable price and the all-in cost in basis points at the
notional in question. The engine ranks, reports and refuses; it computes no fee,
no gas estimate and no impact.

**A venue that cannot fill the notional is excluded and reported**, not ranked
last. "Cannot fill" and "expensive" are different answers.

## Consequences

- **Cost assumptions live where they are known.** A connector knows its fee
  tier; a chain adapter knows its gas basis. Neither is knowable here, and a
  default would be a trading assumption hidden in a comparison helper.
- **The engine is testable without either venue.** Its whole job is the ranking
  and the refusals, and both are exercised with constructed quotes — which is
  also what lets the test for "top of book says one venue, all-in says another"
  exist at all.
- **§18.14's prohibition is enforced by shape.** There is no method that takes
  two mids and returns an arbitrage figure; the executable comparison requires
  a notional, and the mid comparison refuses when a side is an AMM.
- **The caller carries more.** That is the honest split: computing an all-in
  cost is real work with real assumptions, and hiding it inside a median would
  make every downstream number depend on choices nobody recorded.

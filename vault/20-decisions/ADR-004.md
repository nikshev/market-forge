## Context

PRD §8.1 leaves this open. It suggests evaluating `cryptofeed` as a normalized
public-feed adapter, since it already covers trades, L2, funding, open interest
and liquidations across many venues — and then immediately qualifies that:
critical feeds must still have connector-level integration tests against
exchange documentation, sequence gaps and order-book reconstruction rules must
not be hidden, and "native Binance connector may be preferred for Phase 1 if it
yields clearer correctness/auditability".

PRD §35.6 separately requires connector tests to **replay exchange sample
messages** — covering snapshot/delta rules, reconnect, malformed events, rate
limits and time parsing — rather than reaching the network.

## Decision

**A native Binance connector**, not `cryptofeed`.

**Test fixtures are recorded from real Binance traffic once**, committed, and
replayed offline. Tests never open a socket.

## Consequences

- **The rules stay visible.** §8.1's own conditions — do not hide sequence gaps,
  do not hide order-book reconstruction — are conditions on the thing a library
  exists to abstract away. Meeting them through `cryptofeed` would mean
  auditing its internals anyway, and then owning the result without owning the
  code. §11.1's reconstruction procedure is ours to implement and prove.
- **The non-repainting guarantee has no black box under it.** Every claim this
  system makes about `event_time` ordering rests on how the connector assigns
  timestamps and handles gaps. A dependency between the exchange and that
  guarantee is a dependency the guarantee cannot survive.
- **More code, and it is ours to maintain.** Reconnect, keepalive, sequence
  validation and rate limits all have to be written rather than inherited. That
  is the price, and it is paid deliberately.
- **Recorded fixtures beat hand-written samples.** Verified before deciding: a
  live `btcusdt@depth@100ms` message today carries `ps` and `st` fields absent
  from older documentation. A sample written from the docs would have encoded a
  misunderstanding, and the connector would have passed its tests and failed on
  a live stream. Recording captures the format as it is, not as it is described.
- **One network access, then none.** A recording script runs against public
  streams — no credentials — and its output is committed. CI and every
  developer replay those bytes. This satisfies §35.6 and keeps the suite
  deterministic and offline, which the two-gate split (REQ-INFRA-002) needs.
- Reversible: adopting `cryptofeed` later means writing an adapter behind the
  same interface. The fixtures remain valid either way, since they are exchange
  bytes rather than library output.

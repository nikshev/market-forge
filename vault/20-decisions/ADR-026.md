## Context

PRD §16 asks for a z-score four times: funding, open interest, and by
implication anywhere a level needs comparing to its own history. A z-score has
two degenerate cases, and both are common in market data:

- **too few observations** — early in a stream, or after a gap;
- **zero standard deviation** — a funding rate pinned at its floor for a day,
  an open interest figure the venue reports unchanged.

The usual handling is to return `0.0`. That is the worst available answer,
because zero is the *most* meaningful value a z-score can take: it means
"exactly average". A feature that says "exactly average" whenever it has
nothing to say will read as a calm market to every consumer downstream, and
the ones that matter are alerting rules and model inputs.

## Decision

**Both cases refuse**, with the reason named. There is no fallback value.

A caller that wants to proceed without a z-score handles the absence
explicitly, which is a decision made at the call site rather than a default
buried in a statistics helper.

## Consequences

- **Absence is distinguishable from average**, which is the whole point. The
  registry entry for each z-score states this as its null policy, so a
  consumer reads it before using the number.
- **This is the same reasoning as REQ-WP-011's `normalized_delta`**, which is
  absent rather than zero on an empty window, and REQ-WP-006's channel fit,
  which raises rather than fitting on fewer points than asked. The pattern is
  deliberate: a degenerate input produces no answer, never a plausible one.
- **Early in a stream nothing has a z-score**, which is correct and
  occasionally inconvenient. The window length is configurable, so a caller who
  wants earlier numbers chooses a shorter window knowingly rather than
  receiving one silently.
- **A constant series is a data-quality signal.** A venue reporting an
  unchanged funding rate for a week is telling us something; a z-score of zero
  would hide it, and a refusal surfaces it.

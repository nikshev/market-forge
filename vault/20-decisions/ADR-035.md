## Context

PRD §18.7 states the rule and its trap in consecutive sentences:

> Do not reconstruct state by sorting only on timestamp. Canonical event
> ordering is `block_number`, `transaction_index`, `log_index`.

The trap is specific. Every log in a block shares that block's timestamp, and a
busy pool produces several per block — a mint, then a swap through the range it
just created, then a burn. Sorted by timestamp they arrive in whatever order
the sort was stable in, and applying a swap before the mint that supplied its
liquidity yields a state that is wrong in a way nothing downstream flags: the
tick map is plausible, the active liquidity is plausible, and the depth curve
computed from them is simply not the pool's.

The same triple is also the chain's unique identifier for a log. Two records
sharing it are the same log ingested twice — from two providers, or from a
backfill overlapping live ingestion — and applying it twice doubles a mint.

## Decision

**Events are sorted by `(block_number, transaction_index, log_index)`**, and
timestamps take no part in the ordering.

**A repeated triple is refused**, with the block and indices named. Deduplicating
silently would be the friendlier behaviour and would hide the ingestion bug that
produced it.

## Consequences

- **A backfill overlapping live ingestion fails loudly**, which is what should
  happen: the overlap is a configuration mistake, and the alternative is a pool
  whose liquidity is quietly double-counted over the overlapping range.
- **The ordering is a property of the rebuild, not of the caller.** The builder
  sorts what it is given, so a caller who passes events in arrival order gets
  the same answer as one who sorts first — the same reasoning REQ-WP-006's
  fitter uses for its own window.
- **Timestamps remain on the records** and are used for availability
  (REQ-WP-014's `available_at`), which is a different question from ordering.
  Conflating the two is exactly what this ADR separates.
- **Reorged logs are handled upstream.** [[ADR-033]] orphans them in the
  ledger; this adapter reads what the ledger says was knowable, so a reorg
  changes the input rather than needing special handling here.

## Context

REQ-WP-004 requires a "depth-at-bps query". PRD §15.5 asks for "depth within X
bps" and §15.1 names the bands — ±5/10/25/50 bps. Neither says *from what*.

Three references are defensible and give different numbers:

- the **mid price**, `(best_bid + best_ask) / 2`;
- **each side's own best price**, so bid depth is measured from the best bid;
- the **microprice** of §15.2, weighted by opposite queue size.

The difference is not academic. In a wide spread the mid can sit a full band
away from the best bid, so "depth within 5 bps" measured from the mid may
include nothing at all on one side while the same query from the best bid
includes several levels.

## Decision

**The mid price**, and the same reference for both sides.

When either side is empty the mid does not exist, and the query **refuses**
rather than substituting a fallback.

## Consequences

- **One reference means the two sides are comparable.** §15.1's `DI_k` is a
  ratio of bid to ask depth over "fixed distance bands". Measuring each side
  from its own best price would make that ratio a comparison of two different
  distances wearing one name — and the imbalance feature it feeds would move
  with the spread rather than with the liquidity.
- **The microprice is a feature, not a reference.** §15.2 derives it from the
  book; using it to measure the book would make depth depend on queue sizes it
  is supposed to describe.
- **Refusing on an empty side is consistent with the rest of the feature.** PRD
  §11.1 rule 6 already forbids answering from an untrustworthy book. A depth
  measured from a reference that does not exist is the same kind of answer.
- **A caller that wants side-relative depth can still have it.** Top-N levels
  are exposed, and the arithmetic is theirs to do — deliberately, so the choice
  is visible at the call site rather than buried in a default.
- Reversible: the reference is an argument in the query, not a constant in the
  book.
